After `RENAME #old #new`, chathistory acted as if `#new` had just been created. `m_rename.c` (classic RENAME and the relocate form) had no chathistory hook: the rows stayed under `#old`, `#new` started with no history, and `#old`'s rows could no longer be reached once the old name was gone.

This follows the direction in ircv3/ircv3-specifications#532: the channel has always been this name. Its history before the rename is returned under the current name, in one time order with what came after.

**The fix: a mapping, with no rows rewritten.**
- A new RocksDB column family, `renames`, holds one record per current channel name, keyed by the folded name like the other name keys.
- Each entry names an earlier name, the channel's incarnation (its creationtime, which a rename keeps), and the milliseconds between which the channel had that name (since, until).
- The rows that count as the channel's history are those under the earlier name that carry that incarnation and are stamped before the rename. So a channel later created under `#old` (another creationtime) does not mix with the renamed one in either direction. A relocation tombstone (same creationtime) keeps what it stores after the move.
- Records are flat, so nothing is followed at read time. Renaming `#b` to `#c` moves `#b`'s entries into `#c`'s record and adds `#b`. A loop such as `#a→#b→#a` drops the entry for the record's own name.
- A channel arriving at a name does not inherit what that name's record already holds for its incarnation. Those entries belong to another channel created in the same second, or to an earlier stay that the arriving record already covers.
- Each incarnation keeps its 4 latest earlier names, so a read opens at most 5 iterators. A fifth rename drops the oldest name: its rows stay in the store until they age out but are no longer returned, and the server logs which name it stopped following.
- Entries behind the retention floor are dropped. Rows under old names age out exactly as before.
- Every server records the rename itself when it applies it: in `rename_channel()` (local or S2S), `relocate_execute()` and `relocate_again()`. Federation needs no wire change, because `CH Q` names the current name and each store answers from its own record.

**Crossing relocations.** When `#x→#y` and `#x→#z` cross, the channel ends at the name that sorts first. Whichever order a server sees them in, the winner's record ends with every earlier name, each with its original time, and the loser's record keeps none of the moved names. The loser keeps only what it stored itself after the move.

**Read side.** A merged cursor walks the target's own rows together with every earlier-name segment, in key order, and presents each row under the current name. It covers:
- LATEST, BEFORE, AFTER, AROUND, BETWEEN and SEARCH;
- msgid resolution (AROUND/BETWEEN by msgid, REDACT, `CH Q X`);
- reply and redaction lookups;
- TARGETS, including a renamed channel nobody has spoken in since, and a channel renamed away and back (`#a→#b→#a`), whose stamp is the latest row under any of its names;
- the receive-coverage check;
- the burst-loss prune, which also drops the losing incarnation's rows under earlier names.

**Redactions.** A REDACT sent after a rename for a message from before it is stored under the new name, and its original under the old one. The redaction does not depend on the renames record joining the two:
- When a REDACT row is stored, its original is looked for. If it is under another name of the channel, a second reply-index entry is written under that name. The two entries name each other.
- A page reads a message's redaction, and the REDACT row it shows with it, under the name the message is stored under. So the message stays hidden after the REDACT row's name has fallen out of the 4 names the record keeps (`#a→#b`, redact, back to `#a`, four more round trips), and in a relocation's old channel, which reads its own name only.
- The trim engine deletes a redacted original together with its REDACT row. It reads where the original is from the REDACT row's own entry and removes the second entry with it. Before, the cascade looked only under the REDACT row's name, deleted the row, and the message came back.
- The link runs one way: a REDACT sent in a relocation's old channel after the move does not reach the new channel.

**Strict presence** (`CHATHISTORY_STRICT_PRESENCE`, off by default). Presence is kept by channel name and a rename did not touch it. Members of a renamed channel saw none of its history until they rejoined, their intervals stayed open under the old name for whoever took it next, and pre-rename rows were checked against the new name's records. Now:
- `rename_channel()` closes each member's open interval under the old name and opens one under the new. Nothing is copied.
- A row is checked against the presence recorded under the name it is stored under.
- A row with a time but no stored name (a federated one, and the TARGETS stamp) is checked against the name the channel had at that time. A time whose name is not known shows nothing.
- An account's two records change in one write: closed under the old name and open under the new, or neither. A failed read or write leaves the interval where it was and is logged.

**When the store fails.** A read of the renames record that fails makes the query fail (no rows, not the unmapped rows), so no other channel's rows are returned. A write that fails at a rename drops the destination's record, or, if that fails too, marks the name as not to be trusted. That mark is in memory only (a stored one would be a third write to a store that just refused two); a read of the name retries the delete, at most once a second, so the stale record goes as soon as the store takes writes again.

**Known gaps:**
- No RENAME event row is stored or replayed. A RENAME line inside a batch already shown under the new name would tell a `draft/channel-rename` client to rename a buffer it does not have. The record keeps the old name and time if one is wanted later.
- A server that never applied the rename (down or split at the time) has no record, so its `#old` rows do not show under `#new`, and nothing repairs that later. Fixing it needs the burst to carry former names, which is a wire change and out of scope here.
- Two channels created in the same second cannot be told apart by their rows. If a channel is renamed to a name that a dead channel of the same creationtime stored rows under, those rows read as the arriving channel's. The base has the same limit for a channel recreated within the second; the record no longer adds to it.
- Past 4 earlier names the oldest stops being returned (logged, as above). Two renames in the same millisecond are ordered by name when the cap picks which to drop.
- A REDACT stored while its original is not on this store gets no second entry. It holds through the names the record keeps, and no further.
- A REDACT sent in a relocation's old channel after the move, for a message from before it, hides the message there and not in the new channel.
- The records in the `renames` column family are version 2; a version 1 record (written only by earlier pushes of this branch) is not read.
- Read markers and channel metadata are still keyed by the old name.
- Rows from before incarnation stamps (2026-09-08) are not carried, since only rows provably of the renamed channel move.

**Testing:**
- `history_rename_cmocka`, 27 cases, on an in-memory store, and 26 as `history_rename_rocksdb_cmocka` on real RocksDB, including a close and reopen.
  - The cases cover chains, a there-and-back loop, the cap and what it reports, incarnations apart, a same-second arrival, both orders of a crossing, the floor and sweep, limits and cursors across the boundary, a recreated old name, a case-only rename, and read and write errors.
  - Each new case failed against a stub first.
- New suite `history_read_cmocka`, 15 cases: the real `history.o` with the trim engine and RocksDB. Rows go in through `history_store_message()` and a rename through `history_channel_renamed()`; pages, msgid and redaction lookups, context, the TARGETS stamp, the prune and the trim cascade are read back through `history.c`. A page is read the way `m_chathistory.c` builds it: context attached, then the page's redaction filter.
  - With the round-2 fixes switched off, the TARGETS case and both trim cases fail (the redacted message comes back).
  - The three redaction cases (past the names cap, the relocation's old channel, trim past the cap) failed before the second entry was written.
- `history_trim_cmocka`: 6 new cases for the REDACT cascade across names (3 by the callback, 3 by the entry the REDACT row carries), red then green.
- `rename_handlers_cmocka`: both orders of a crossing; the loser-first order failed before.
- `chathistory_presence_cmocka`: 3 new cases (intervals move at a rename, the filter follows it, an unknown name shows nothing); two failed before.
- New suite `chathistory_presence_store_cmocka`: the same source on the in-memory store, with account records. A rename whose write fails, and one whose read fails, leave the account's interval under the old name only; both failed before.
- The full `make cmocka && make test-cmocka` passes: 99 suites, built with `-Werror=implicit-function-declaration`.
- Live, one server:
  - Three messages in `#old`, `RENAME #old #new`, two more: `CHATHISTORY LATEST #new * 50` returns all five as `#new`, in order, and BEFORE pages across the rename. The base build returns two.
  - REDACT from `#new` of a message stored under `#old` works.
  - After `#a→#b→#a`, TARGETS lists `#a` with the time of its latest row. The first version of this branch did not.
  - After four renames the first name's row is still returned; after the fifth it is not, and the warning is logged.
  - `#a→#b`, REDACT in `#b` of a message from `#a`, back to `#a`, four round trips through other names (the drop of `#b` is logged): LATEST `#a` still hides the message. The second version of this branch showed it again.
- Live, strict presence on: a member all along sees rows from both sides of the rename under the new name; one who joined part-way sees only what they were present for, across the rename; one who joined after the rename sees nothing from before it; and a member of the renamed channel does not count as present in the next channel to take the old name. The first version of this branch failed all but the third.
- Live, two linked servers with `RENAME_CONSENT`:
  - Crossing relocations, with each server's own store answering alone (the link cut): the server that ran the winning rename first and the one that ran the losing rename first both return the full history under the winner. The loser's name returns none of it on the loser-first server; on the winner-first server a client cannot read it (the join is redirected). The first version left the moved rows readable under the loser.
  - A relocation `#old→#new`, then REDACT in `#new` of a message from before the move: a member still in `#old` no longer gets the message, and still gets the rest. The second version of this branch showed it.
- Not checked live: trim eviction of a redacted message across a rename (covered by `history_read_cmocka` on RocksDB), and a failing store (the retry of a distrusted record's delete has no test: `history.c` is linked only against RocksDB in the suites, which has no fault injection).
