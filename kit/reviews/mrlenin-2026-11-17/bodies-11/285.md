[SASL 3.1](https://ircv3.net/specs/extensions/sasl-3.1): "If the client completes registration (with CAP END, NICK, USER and any other necessary messages) while the SASL authentication is still in progress, the server SHOULD abort it and send a 906 numeric, then register the client without authentication."

An exchange in flight holds registration, so a client that sent `AUTHENTICATE PLAIN` and then NICK, USER and CAP END got nothing until the SASL timeout.

### What changes

- **Abort at registration.** `check_auth_finished()` aborts the exchange when the SASL hold is the only thing registration still waits for, and the exchange waits for the client (`sasl_exchange_awaits_client()`). The abort is the one `AUTHENTICATE *` runs: 906 (904 after a local error challenge), the backend is told, and registration goes on.
- **Whose turn it is.**
  - A local (Keycloak) exchange is the client's turn unless a Keycloak request is in flight.
  - A services or IAuth exchange is the client's turn once the backend has answered every message the client sent. `con_sasl_unanswered` counts them. A `+` that answers the mechanism doesn't hand the turn back while pipelined credentials are still with the backend.
  - A message of 400 bytes or more travels as several lines and counts once, on its last line, in both directions.
- **A challenge owes a check.** When a challenge hands the turn back (services, IAuth, or a Keycloak answer), `sasl_client_turn_check()` queues a registration check. A client that registered while the backend had its request isn't left waiting for the timeout.
- **A completing login isn't aborted.** `sasl_login_apply()` marks the exchange `SASLX_COMPLETING`. A local mechanism completes inside the client's own AUTHENTICATE, while the exchange still looks like the client's turn, and the registration check in the login's own step 3 must not abort it.

An exchange whose request is with the backend still holds registration until the answer, as before.

### Tests

- **CMocka `sasl_turn_cmocka`:** whose turn, the unanswered count, where a message ends.
- **CMocka `sasl_handler_cmocka`:** the abort in each tier, the backend's turn, pipelined credentials, a chunked response, a completing login is not aborted.
- **CMocka `auth_recheck_cmocka`:** the check aborts only when registration is otherwise complete.
- **CMocka `services_reply_cmocka`:** a services challenge owes a check, but not one that answers the mechanism ahead of pipelined credentials, nor the first line of a chunked challenge.
- **The whole CMocka suite passes.**
- **irctest** (Nefarious controller with X3): `testRegistrationDuringSasl` now passes. The full runs with and without X3 show no new failures.

**Changed after review:** the abort at registration is the server's, not the client's: an abort of data not yet judged no longer counts toward `SASL_MAX_FAILURES`, so it can never close the connection the client is completing (SASL 3.1 registers that client without authentication). `AUTHENTICATE *` still counts as before. CMocka `sasl_handler_cmocka`: `registration_abort_is_not_a_failure`.

**Changed after a second review, then simplified:** a client that pipelines AUTHENTICATE PLAIN and CAP END gets the challenge, then 906, and registers. Its credentials line then arrives after registration and could have started a new exchange with the base64 credentials as the mechanism name, sent on to IAuth or services. The first AUTHENTICATE of an exchange must now be an RFC 4422 mechanism name (upper case, at most 24 characters, for ECDSA-NIST256P-CHALLENGE); anything else gets 908 and 904 and reaches no backend. A client that names an advertised mechanism in lower case (`plain`) is accepted, and the exchange starts with the advertised spelling.

**Changed after review (re-evaluation):** a client that names an advertised mechanism in lower case (`AUTHENTICATE plain`) is accepted again. The line that starts an exchange must still be an RFC 4422 mechanism name; one that is not is compared, ignoring case, with the list the `sasl` capability advertises, and on a match the exchange starts with the list's own spelling, so IAuth or services are only ever sent a name out of the server's list, never the client's bytes. Everything else still gets 908 and 904 and reaches no backend. Withholding a challenge from a client whose registration is about to abort stays removed: it changed only which numerics that client saw, and the credentials it guarded are refused by the check above.

**Changed after the second review:** a refused OAUTHBEARER token is counted whichever way its exchange ends. The count is taken when the error challenge is delivered, once per exchange, so a client that then registers instead of sending `*` was already counted; the `!for_registration` exception in `authenticate_end()` changed nothing and is removed. `sasl_handler_cmocka` now covers CAP END after a refusal. Not changed: with no mechanism list known, `AUTHENTICATE plain` still gets a bare 904 without 908.

**Deploy:** a client that sends `CAP END` before its credentials, and whose backend's challenge arrives first, now gets 906 and registers without a login, where the old build held registration and logged it in. That is the spec's behaviour, but such a client loses its login. Correctly ordered pipelining is unaffected.
