Reviewed at fce9e8b9. Built at the head, the CMocka suites pass (96 suites), and base and head were run on a scratch server with a bcrypt Operator password; ASan, irctest and OPER from a bouncer device were not run.

The code is correct. Run: `OPER op pw`, `PING :x` and `WHOIS` of yourself in one write. The base answers `PONG` and the whole WHOIS (without 313) before MODE +o and 381; a wrong password, `PONG` before 464; SETHOST, `PONG` before 396. The head answers 381 and MODE, then `PONG`, then WHOIS with 313; 464 and 396 come before `PONG`; two OPERs back to back both run in order (base: "already in progress"). Every way the wait could fail to end was traced (refused submit, client exit, bouncer hold and revive, REHASH, an answer for another connection on a reused fd); none leaves it held.

1. Low, by reading: nothing ends the wait if a check never answers (a stuck worker, an absurd hash cost in ircd.conf). With the 10 s bound removed, that client's lines wait forever and it may ping out on its own side. A log line, or a backstop at e.g. 30 s, would make a stuck worker visible instead of a silently frozen client.
